# The picture behind any creative

The design language every image prompt inherits. A format guide adds its
own composition; the brand fills the slots; the script assembles them.

## Slots the brand fills (brand/visual-identity.md, Imagery)

{subject}     what the owner's pictures show: the work, the place, the product, the material, the hands
{setting}     where it happens: the place of work, the vehicle, the customer's home, the counter, the site
{light}       the light the brand's pictures have: north window, overcast, one lamp, dawn; never studio flash
{materials}   the materials and objects that carry the brand, including the one that carries its colour
{people}      the people rule: none; hands and backs only; the owner by name with permission; customers with permission
{never}       what the brand never shows
{anchor}      the style anchor paragraph, reused at the end of every prompt

The brief fills {offer} (what this campaign sells) and {audience_place}
(where the audience is when the problem is felt). The format guide fills
the rest.

## Composition

- One subject, one moment, mid-action or just after. A scene with three
  things in it is a scene with none.
- The camera is where a person would stand: eye level and a little to the
  side for work; above and close for materials; from the doorway for a
  room. Not a drone, not a fisheye, not dead centre.
- The frame keeps the copy-space the format names: a plain wall, a bare
  bench, a sky, a floor. The words go there through the template; the
  picture leaves it empty.
- Wear is allowed and wanted: a scuff, dust in the light, a mug half
  drunk, a pencil. A spotless scene reads as stock.

## Light and colour

- One light source the brand's pictures have ({light}). Soft shadows
  that fall one way. No rim light, no glow, no flare, no HDR.
- Colour lives in the materials ({materials}) and on one object that
  carries the brand's colour. Never a tint over the whole scene, never a
  gradient sky, never purple and teal.
- The palette is what the setting actually has: wood, steel, plaster,
  paint, fabric, skin, sky.

## Mood, as visible properties only

Say what the camera can see. Not "warm and trustworthy" but "soft
shadows, a little dust in the light, nothing staged". Not "premium" but
"one object, a lot of empty bench, even light". Adjectives without a
visible property produce the average picture.

## People

{people}. When people appear: real clothes, real posture, doing the thing,
never looking at the camera, never posed, never a stock smile. Customers
only with permission and never as an invented result.

## What to say instead of what to omit

Models follow "an empty street" better than "no cars". Write the empty
version of the thing: "the surfaces are bare except the pencil", "the room
is empty of people", "the wall is plain painted plaster".

## Never

{never}. Also never: painted text, logos, numbers, arrows, charts or
interfaces (the template does those); a stranger smiling at the camera; a
handshake; a pointing finger; a laptop with coffee; a headset; a team
high-five; plastic skin; extra fingers; symmetry and centring by default;
a subject floating on a gradient; the words "cinematic", "8k",
"ultra-detailed", "trending" in the prompt.
