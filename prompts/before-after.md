# Before and after

Two pictures, one difference. The eye finds the change before the reader
decides whether to read; that only works when the two frames match in
everything except the content.

## Composition for this format
- Same camera position, same lens, same crop, same light, same time of
  day in both frames. Say "the same angle and the same light" in both
  prompts.
- The before shows the problem plainly and without contempt: it is the
  reader's own situation. Worn, cluttered, tired, but lit the same as the
  after.
- The after shows the work done and nothing added that the work did not
  do. No brighter grade, no added sun, no props the before did not have.
- Copy-space: none inside the frames; the template places the labels.
- Real pairs from the owner first. A generated pair is illustrative; the
  record's notes say so, and the copy claims only what the ledger holds.

## Prompt template, before
{setting} before the work: the {subject} as the reader lives with it now,
worn and used, shot from the doorway at eye level. {light}. {materials}
as they are before. The scene is empty of people. The same angle and the
same light as the after. {never}. {anchor}

## Prompt template, after
{setting} after the work: the same room from the same doorway at the same
eye level, the {subject} finished, everything that was there still there
and in the same place except what the work changed. {light}, the same
light as the before. {materials}, finished. The scene is empty of people.
{never}. {anchor}

## When the change is in a person
Never generate a body, a face or a skin change. Show the change in the
life instead: the same {setting} before and after with what the person
does differently in it (what is on the counter, on the desk, by the
door). The claim stays in the copy and cites the ledger; a result most
customers will not get says what is typical.

## Hooks that fit this picture
"[What it was] to [what it is], [time or place]"; the number of days or
hours between the two frames; the reader's own before described in
their words.

## Policy
Meta allows before/after only for general cosmetic use, 18+, no close-ups
implying a flaw; health, weight and skincare are restricted; Pinterest
bans the format outright. Faking the after of a real job is never done.
