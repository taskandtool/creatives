# Photo with a statement

The picture is the proof of the headline; the template's scrim band takes
the words. Used by problem-agitate-solve, outcome, who-it's-for and any
statement that needs a scene.

## Composition for this format
- The subject is the outcome the headline claims, shown just after it
  happened: the finished {subject} in its {setting}, the tools packed or
  the last step still visible.
- Camera at eye level, a little to the side, close enough that the
  material reads.
- Copy-space: the lower third of the frame is a plain surface ({setting}'s
  plainest wall, bench or floor) with nothing on it. The band sits there.
- A 9:16 story wants the plain area in the middle third instead; a 1.91:1
  banner wants it on the left half.

## Prompt template
{subject} in {setting}, just after the work is done, one detail of the
work still in frame. Camera at eye level and a little to the side, close
enough to read the material. {light}. {materials}; the brand's colour on
one object only. The lower third of the frame is a plain surface with
nothing on it. Soft shadows falling one way, a little wear, nothing
staged. {people}. {never}. {anchor}

## Hooks that fit this picture
The outcome as a fact ("Fitted in three days."), the reader's problem as
a question they would ask, the number from the claims ledger.

## Pitfalls
A picture that needs the headline to explain it; the subject centred on
a plain ground with nothing around it (that is the product format); a
person looking at the camera.
